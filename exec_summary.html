Validation of Measurement Modules (IP15)

Compliance

Table 1. Respondent Compliance in waist and hip measurement modules
Self-Measurement Self-Measurement w/ Interviewer BVI App
Compliance Rate [Waist] 56.2% (n=1683) 95.5% (n=22) 22.2% (n=1565)
Compliance Rate [Hip] 54.2% (n=1683) 95.5% (n=22) 7.9% (n=1565)

 

Table 1 reports the compliance rate across the three modes of data collection. These initial results suggest that self-measurement with an interviewer present elicits a considerably higher compliance rate (95.5%) than the other two modes of collection: 39pp higher than waist self-measurement in CAWI respondents, and 73pp more than respondents who were assigned the BVI app for waist measurement.

However, we only have data on interviewer-led, self-measurement for 22 respondents, which warrants caution in interpreting these results.

The BVI app shows the lowest respondent uptake, at only 22.2% for waist circumference and 7.9% for hip circumference. Out of the 1565 respondents who were asked to download and use the BVI app, 497 (ie. 32%) reported to have installed and logged in successfully. However, only 347 respondents (nearly 70%) send back usable waist data, and only 123 (around 25%) respondents showed valid hip measurements.

At this stage it’s not clear what is the cause of the missing data on waist (Nmissing=150) and hip (Nmissing=224) measurements, but it certainly conditions our ability to interpret the compliance rate of installing and using the app, as almost 1/3 of the respondents who report having installing and logging in successfully don’t send back valid waist and hip data.

The BVI app data seems to provide less precise estimates for waist circumference when compared to self-measurements with or without the interviewer present (the standard deviation for waist is twice as large in the BVI app compared to the other two).

Furthermore, the difference between the average waist in the BVI app data compared to the interviewer-monitored self-measurement is just over 20cm, and almost 30cm compared to the self-measurement group. However, these three groups are not so different in terms of the average weight: approximately 80kg in the self-measurement group, 80kg in the BVI app group and 87kg in the interviewer-led measurement group.

Therefore, the large differences we find between the BVI app waist measurements and the other two measurements are probably driven by the hurdles of collecting data through the app. The wide standard deviation corroborate the lower efficiency of waist and hip estimates obtained through the app.

However, one caveat to this interpretation is the large number of outliers in the BVI app data when compared to the other analogue modes of self-measurement, which can explain differences at the mean. Notwithstanding, the values reported in Table 2 are computed after removing one outlier for which the waist circumference was 2000cm as reported by the BVI app. The long form of this report provides more granular results on this point.

The self-measurement monitored by the interviewer works as a barometer for the quality of the data gathered, as it offers both more reliability and more precision than any of the other two measurements. Unfortunately, we have a small N for CAPI respondents (N=21), those who agreed to measure their waist and hip under the supervision of an interviewer, which dampens our confidence in the results of these comparisons.

Differences in Waist measurements

Consistent with earlier results, the BVI app seems to yield the worst quality data: the average difference in measurements for respondents who used both the tape and the BVI app to measure their waist circumference is around 28cm. The average difference for those respondents who measured with the app and the interviewer present is 17cm. And although the N is small for the latter comparison (only 5 respondents), that is certainly not the case for the former (N=231).

Similar differences in Hip measurements…

  • between interviewer-led and self-measurement, the average difference in hip circumference is 25cm and in waist-to-hip ratio is 0.04.

  • between the BVI app and tape measurements, the average difference in hip circumference is 11.5 cm (without interviewer present) and 6.8 (with interviewer present).

And in Waist-to-Hip ratios

With respect to waist-to-hip ratios, the BVI app bias is 3 times higher than the self-measurement without interviewer present, when we compare both to interviewer-led measurements of waist-to-hip ratio.

 

Take home points

 

  1. When comparing self-measurement and BVI app data against interviewer-led measurements, we find that the bias is quite small for self-measurement without the interviewer and quite large for the BVI app;

  2. BVI app data tends to be less precise overall in its estimates;

  3. The bias in waist measurements tends to be considerably higher than in hip measurements (2 and a half times higher when comparing BVI app with interviewer-led self-measurement).

  4. The lower quality of the BVI app data may be driven by several factors, including the setup of the room where the photos were taken. Only 30% of the respondents who gave us valid BVI data took a picture with an empty wall in the background, and out of those only 62% (64 respondents) did it in front of a white wall. In short, only 18.4% of the sub-sample with valid BVI app estimates took the pictures in the best possible conditions.
